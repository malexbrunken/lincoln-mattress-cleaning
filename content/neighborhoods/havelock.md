## How did a railroad town shape Havelock's bedrooms?

Havelock grew up around the railroad. The Burlington broke ground on its shops in June 1890 and had 400 men on the payroll by 1895, and the Lancaster Land Company put up the Lancaster Block, still standing at 6201–6205 Havelock Avenue, in the middle of the planned business district at about the same time. The 1922 shop strike stalled the town, and in 1930 its voters approved annexation by Lincoln.

The census tract that covers most of Havelock still carries that history. Its median home was built in 1955, 39% of homes date from before 1940 and only 4% from 2000 or later, against 28% citywide. Two-bedroom homes are the most common size at 36%, compared with 28% across Lincoln, yet 21% have four bedrooms, more than Lincoln's 17%.

## What does that mean for a mattress visit?

Often a mix of beds in one house: a main bedroom, a kids' room and sometimes a basement bedroom. We clean each mattress where it sits, so tight corners and narrow stairs don't matter. Every visit is a material inspection, low-moisture dry vapor steam on the surface, seams and tufts, HEPA vacuuming and UV-C light treatment, plus two checks: a moisture check after the job and the built-in bed mite sensor on our UV-C vacuum.

## Where does humidity matter in an older Havelock house?

In the basement and the top floor. Lincoln Airport's normals put the average July dew point near 65°F, and LLCHD recommends 30 to 50 percent humidity indoors. A cool basement bedroom is where summer moisture shows up first, so a dehumidifier earns its keep. Our [Lincoln humidity and bed mites guide](/allergy-season/lincoln-humidity-and-bed-mites) has the local ranges, and the science of heat and moisture is in the [Sleep Sanitation Knowledge Center](https://sleepsanitation.com/knowledge-center).
