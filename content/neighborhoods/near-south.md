## Why does Near South need its own plan?

Because the same neighborhood holds two kinds of bedroom. In the tract around Mount Emerald, only 13% of homes are single-family detached houses; 51% are in buildings of two to nine units, against 12% citywide. That is the footprint of a neighborhood where large houses were divided into apartments and small walk-ups filled in the gaps: 28% of that tract's homes were built in the 1970s. More than half are studios or one-bedrooms, so the typical bed is a single queen or full in a compact room.

South of there the picture flips. In tract 23, 59% of homes are detached houses, 57% were built before 1940, and 53% have three or more bedrooms. A visit there often means two or three beds in one house: a main bedroom plus children's or guest rooms upstairs.

## What does the National Register record tell you about the rooms?

The 1980 nomination for the Mount Emerald and Capitol Additions district describes about 228 dwellings on roughly 20 blocks between A and G Streets and 17th and 22nd Streets, with brick alleys, rear garages and many houses converted into apartments. For a mattress visit that often means an upper-floor unit reached by an older staircase. That doesn't change our method, because the mattress is cleaned in place: low-moisture dry vapor steam, HEPA vacuuming and UV-C light treatment on the surface, seams and tufts.

## How do older rooms change the moisture side?

Old houses vary room by room. Lincoln Airport's average July dew point is about 65°F, while LLCHD recommends 30 to 50 percent humidity indoors. An attic-level bedroom without good air conditioning can sit well above that range in summer. A hygrometer tells you where yours is, and our [Lincoln humidity and bed mites guide](/allergy-season/lincoln-humidity-and-bed-mites) explains what LLCHD and Nebraska Extension recommend. After a cleaning, the bed stays unmade until it is dry to the touch, and we run a moisture check before we leave.
