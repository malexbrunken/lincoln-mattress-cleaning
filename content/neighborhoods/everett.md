## Why is Everett two neighborhoods for a mattress visit?

Because its two census tracts look nothing alike. The tract that holds Everett School is an apartment district: 48% of homes are in buildings of ten or more units, only 18% are detached houses, and the most common home is a one-bedroom. The neighboring tract is a house district: 69% detached, 58% built before 1940, and two-bedroom homes are the most common size. Citywide, Lincoln's most common home has three bedrooms.

So an Everett booking is usually one of two jobs: a single bed in a compact apartment, or two or three beds in an old house.

## What makes Everett's houses different from the rest of Lincoln?

Age. The Everett Neighborhood Association traces the area to the 1867 Original Plat north of A Street and to South Lincoln Addition (1869) and Dawson's Addition (1870) south of it, and says some of Lincoln's oldest houses, from the 1870s, stand here. Bahr Terrace and Lyman Terrace near 11th and H, built around 1889, are described as the two finest examples of early row houses in Lincoln. Only 12% of Lincoln's housing dates from before 1940; in the house tract here it is 58%.

Old houses have small, varied rooms and upstairs bedrooms, and none of that matters to our method, because we clean the mattress where it is: low-moisture dry vapor steam, HEPA vacuuming and UV-C light treatment on the surface, seams and tufts.

## How should an Everett household think about summer humidity?

Room by room. Lincoln Airport's average July dew point is about 65°F, and LLCHD recommends keeping indoor humidity between 30 and 50 percent. Older upper floors and basements can drift outside that range. Our [Lincoln humidity and bed mites guide](/allergy-season/lincoln-humidity-and-bed-mites) has the local numbers. After a visit the bed stays unmade until it is dry to the touch, and we run a moisture check after the job.

## What if the mattress should go instead?

Ask the association about its spring and fall clean-ups, or use the city's North 48th Street transfer station any time; our [move-out and disposal guide](/academic-year/may-move-out-and-disposal) explains the covered-load rule. If the bed is sound, cleaning is the cheaper reset. The method is explained in the [Sleep Sanitation Knowledge Center](https://sleepsanitation.com/knowledge-center).
