## How is the Highlands different from Lincoln's older neighborhoods?

It is young. In the Highlands census tract, 46% of homes were built in the 1990s, against 13% citywide, and another 23% since 2000. Only 2% predate 1960, where in Country Club or Havelock most homes do. Seventy percent are detached houses, and the three-bedroom house is the norm: 48% of homes, compared with 32% across Lincoln. Two-bedroom homes, the most common size in Havelock, are just 11% here.

So a typical Highlands house has a main bedroom plus two more bedrooms for kids or guests, which is three mattresses in one visit.

## What does a whole-house visit include?

Every mattress gets a material inspection, low-moisture dry vapor steam on the surface, seams and tufts, HEPA vacuuming and UV-C light treatment, with enzyme treatment for urine. The visit includes two checks: a moisture check after the job and the built-in bed mite sensor on our UV-C vacuum. Why heat and low moisture work is explained in the [Sleep Sanitation Knowledge Center](https://sleepsanitation.com/knowledge-center).

## Does living next to the airport's weather station tell you anything?

It tells you the outdoor numbers are about as local as they get. NOAA's Lincoln normals are measured at Lincoln Airport, about a mile from the neighborhood: an average dew point near 65°F in July and 17°F in January. LLCHD recommends 30 to 50 percent humidity indoors. A hygrometer shows where each room sits; a finished basement bedroom is the room to watch in summer. Our [Lincoln humidity and bed mites guide](/allergy-season/lincoln-humidity-and-bed-mites) has the details.
