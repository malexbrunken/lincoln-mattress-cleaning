## The Lincoln license

The City Council adopted Lincoln's short-term rental rules on June 14, 2021, and they took effect September 20, 2021. A short-term rental is any rental of all or part of a dwelling for stays of no more than 30 consecutive days. The rules, per Building and Safety and the Planning Department:

- **One license per unit.** $250 per rental unit, renewed every year, issued by Building and Safety at 555 South 10th Street.
- **License before listing.** Advertising a Lincoln short-term rental without a license is unlawful.
- **Zoning.** Short-term rentals are a conditional permitted use in the AG, AGR, R-1 through R-8, O-1 through O-3, R-T, B-1 through B-5 and H-1 through H-4 districts.
- **Multi-family buildings.** In R-1 through R-8, a multi-family property may license at least one unit but no more than 10% of its units.
- **No signs** in the AG, AGR and R-1 through R-8 districts.
- **Accessory dwellings** may be used as short-term rentals.
- **Emergency contact.** The application asks for someone who can reach the property within 45 minutes.

The city points to Lincoln Municipal Code chapter 5.39 and sections 27.02.200, 27.06.070 and 27.62.040 for the full text. Questions go to Building and Safety at 402-441-7521.

## The guest cap, sleeping area by sleeping area

Lincoln's cap is two people per sleeping area, up to 12 per rental. A sleeping area is habitable space with at least one working emergency escape and rescue opening, an egress window. Bedrooms count, other rooms can count if they meet egress standards, and hallways, kitchens and bathrooms never do. A three-bedroom house with egress windows in each bedroom tops out at six guests, however many beds you put in it, unless another room also meets the egress standard.

We quote a multi-bed Lincoln turnover per mattress: the first at $249, or $199 under the Fall 2026 first-mattress offer, then $199 for each additional full, queen or king and $149 for a kids bed.

## The taxes, stacked

The city's own example, from its 2023 FAQ, for a $500 Lincoln stay, using the rates in that FAQ:

| Line | Rate | Amount | Paid to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rental rate | | $500.00 | |
| Lincoln short-term rental occupation tax | 4% | $20.00 | City, through Host Compliance |
| State and city sales tax | 5.5% + 1.75% | $37.70 | Nebraska Department of Revenue |
| State and county lodging tax | 1% + 4% | $26.00 | Nebraska Department of Revenue |
| Total | | $583.70 | |

Sales and lodging taxes are figured on $520, the rent plus the city tax. Lancaster County's 4% lodging tax has been in place since July 1, 2005, according to the Department of Revenue's county table, on top of the 1% state lodging tax.

Airbnb's Nebraska tax page says it collects state and local sales tax and state and county lodging tax on stays of 29 nights and shorter, including cleaning and guest fees. It has a separate entry for Omaha's city tax and none for Lincoln, which matches the city FAQ's statement that Airbnb doesn't collect Lincoln's 4%. City filings are due by the 25th for the previous month. Interest runs at 1% per month on late payments, and the Finance Director may add a 10% penalty. The tax took effect November 1, 2021.

One change is coming. The Treasurer's Occupation Taxes page says information on new short-term rental software is "coming soon," so the Host Compliance filing step may change. This page isn't legal or tax advice; the City Treasurer's line is (402) 441-7457.

## What we don't know

- **The replacement for Host Compliance.** The city hasn't said what or when.
- **Whether Airbnb's 29-night threshold and Lincoln's 30-day definition ever disagree on your stay.** Ask the Department of Revenue at (402) 471-5729 if a booking lands on day 30.
