## Lincoln Airport's wind year

Our monthly averages of NOAA's 1991 to 2020 hourly normals for Lincoln Airport:

| Month | Average wind | Prevailing from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| January | 9.2 mph | North |
| February | 9.8 mph | North |
| March | 10.9 mph | North |
| April | 11.8 mph | North |
| May | 10.5 mph | South |
| June | 9.8 mph | South |
| July | 8.7 mph | South |
| August | 8.3 mph | South |
| September | 9.2 mph | South |
| October | 9.5 mph | South |
| November | 9.5 mph | South |
| December | 8.9 mph | North |

The switch from a north to a south wind lands in May, and August is the calmest month on average at 8.3 mph.

## Airborne pollen and sticky pollen

LLCHD's air quality page separates two kinds. Sticky, larger grains travel on a carrier, such as animal fur, hair, feathers, insects or clothing, and seldom stay in the air long. Airborne grains are tiny, and the smallest can be carried on the wind for many miles. Bolick's 2001 State Museum note added that Nebraska's strong winds can strip even sticky grains from plants.

## What this means for the bedroom

The wind brings the airborne kind to the window, and you, the dog and your clothes bring the sticky kind to the bed. Extension's G2069 points at the openings: quality doors, windows and screens, holes fixed, and windows closed with the air conditioning on in peak seasons.
