## How does Lincoln's wind change through the year?

Our monthly averages of NOAA's 1991 to 2020 hourly normals for Lincoln Airport:

| Month | Average wind | Prevailing from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| January | 9.2 mph | North |
| February | 9.8 mph | North |
| March | 10.9 mph | North |
| April | 11.8 mph | North |
| May | 10.5 mph | South |
| June | 9.8 mph | South |
| July | 8.7 mph | South |
| August | 8.3 mph | South |
| September | 9.2 mph | South |
| October | 9.5 mph | South |
| November | 9.5 mph | South |
| December | 8.9 mph | North |

At Lincoln Airport the switch from a north to a south wind lands in May, and August is the calmest month at 8.3 mph.

## What is the difference between airborne and sticky pollen?

LLCHD's air quality page separates two kinds: sticky, larger grains that ride a carrier such as animal fur, hair, feathers, insects or clothing and seldom stay airborne long, and tiny airborne grains, the smallest of which LLCHD says can travel on the wind for many miles. Bolick's 2001 State Museum note added that Nebraska's strong winds can strip even sticky grains from plants.

## What does this mean for the bedroom?

In March and April, Lincoln Airport's windiest months, the airborne kind reaches the window screen; the sticky kind reaches the bed on you, the dog and your clothes, and LLCHD's home list separately says to keep pets out of sleeping areas.
