## Why the two ranges differ

LLCHD's air quality page gives 30 to 50 percent as the generally recommended relative humidity for homes, warning that air too dry irritates airways and air too humid feeds biological pollutants; it adds that house dust mites grow in damp, warm environments. Extension's G2069 is narrower because it targets the mites themselves: they live in warm, humid places and in soft furnishings such as mattresses, pillows and bedcovers, and it says to reduce humidity to 30 to 45 percent.

## Lincoln Airport's dew point, month by month

Our monthly averages of NOAA's 1991 to 2020 hourly normals:

| Month | Dew point | Month | Dew point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January | 17°F | July | 65°F |
| February | 20°F | August | 64°F |
| March | 28°F | September | 54°F |
| April | 38°F | October | 40°F |
| May | 50°F | November | 29°F |
| June | 60°F | December | 20°F |

The dew point is the outdoor moisture figure; when 65°F July air is cooled in a bedroom, its relative humidity rises, which is how a Lincoln room passes 45 percent without any spill or leak.

## Extension's bedding steps

G2069 lists covers labeled for dust mite control on the mattress, box spring and pillows, all bedding washed weekly in 130°F water and dried hot, and hard surfaces in the bedroom for easier cleaning. Our [Lincoln guide to bed mites](/guides/dust-mites-in-lincoln-mattresses) covers what heat did in two published steam studies.
