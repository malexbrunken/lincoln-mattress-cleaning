Most cleaning services ask you to judge the result by eye and by nose. Both are unreliable on a mattress, for the same reason: you cannot see into quilting and seam channels, and a nose adapts to a room within minutes.

So we brought an instrument.

## The sequence

Our process finishes with UV-C post-treatment, and the order matters. Steam does the lifting and the heat work; the UV-C pass follows on the cleaner surface. Running UV-C first would mean shining it at soil, which is not where it does the most good.

The machine we use is a UV-C mattress vacuum that also carries a surface reader. We take a reading before treatment begins and another after the steam and UV-C passes are complete.

## What the number means — and what it does not

**What it is:** a relative measure of contamination present on the surface at the point of measurement. It is a verification tool for our own work. It tells us — and you — whether the surface we just treated actually changed.

**What it is not:** a medical instrument. It is not a diagnosis, not an allergen test, and not a pest-detection device. If you have an active bed bug infestation, this reading does not tell you that, and we do not use it that way.

## A real job

On a recent appointment the machine read high on the pre-treatment surface. After the dry vapor steam pass and the UV-C treatment, the same machine on the same mattress read **0**.

Both readings come from the same device within the same appointment, so there is no comparison fudging — it is a before and after on one mattress, photographed. That pair is in our [gallery](/gallery).

## Why we photograph it

Because "we cleaned it thoroughly" is a claim, and a reading is evidence. If a service is worth $299, it is worth showing you the change rather than describing it.

You get both readings on request, on every appointment. We do not charge for it, and it is not an upsell — UV-C post-treatment is included in the base mattress price for every customer, at every property.
